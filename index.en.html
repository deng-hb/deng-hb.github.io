Open Source

Open-source works

It began with one simple Hello World — turning imagination into interfaces that breathe. What follows are open-source tools refined again and again on Java and Spring Boot.

01

EORM

Keep SQL in your own hands. A lightweight persistence layer that starts with three annotations, where lambda field references hold their ground at compile time; every statement passes through review — transparent, restrained, and sharp enough.

02

E-R.Design

Give your data model version and order. Multi-version ER modeling, visual schema editing, one-click migration DDL; reverse-engineer models from live databases, with permissions down to every single table.

03

jpa-spec-chain

Write JPA queries with method references. Type-safe joins, projections and aggregations; joins are reused automatically, paging and count come along; and when you need it, it is still a plain Specification.

04

running-line

See the lines your code actually ran. The agent plants line probes at class load and records every hit and its timing; the console restores source by commit, colors each line, and branch diffs leave uncovered changes nowhere to hide.

05

Tilde Secret

A quiet guard for your APIs. Requests are encrypted with one-time keys, responses are signed, and the Filter handles the negotiation in silence; not a line of business code changes, yet it is protected.

Essay

Slow down, let the light catch up

Essay·Late night·~1,000 words

Sending a line of text thousands of miles takes no longer than a heartbeat. Yet I always want it slower — slow enough that you pause for half a second while scrolling, slow enough that between the blinks of the caret you can hear your own breathing.

In my earlier years I worshipped speed: smaller bundles, faster loads, shorter waits. Only later did I understand that speed is courtesy, not the whole of it. What people remember are the details that seem unnecessary: a movement that lands exactly right, a gradient that never steals the show, a border that lights up softly as you come near. They solve nothing, yet they turn a passing visit into the feeling of being hosted.

Speed is courtesy, not the whole of it; style is patience, placed into detail inch by inch. — On “just right”

A web page is the only kind of paper that breathes on its own. It has weight — not in grams, but in the minutes someone chooses to stay; it has temperature — not in kelvins, but in the unhurried patience between the lines. I call these invisible things style. They cannot be compressed, only placed in, one bit at a time: the hesitation of a punctuation mark, the measure of a line of whitespace, and the exact quiet that arrives when you reach the bottom.

That is why this page borrows no framework. Not to prove anything — simply to run my hands along the curve of every line, the way a craftsman treats a piece of wood. Fewer tools mean fuller attention; fuller attention is what lets detail show itself.

There is an advantage to writing at night: the world goes quiet and the screen becomes the only source of light. You type at it, it hands the words back, and neither side is in a hurry. Many ideas develop only in moments of mutual regard like that.

If you happened to pass by, I hope these few minutes feel light to you. You need not remember anything; a single small stir of “so it could be like this” before you close the tab is enough.
